An intensive, hands-on plan to go from zero (or rusty) to confidently modeling real parts in parametric 3D CAD in a single day. Recommended software: Autodesk Fusion 360 — free for personal use, beginner-friendly, industry-relevant. Alternatives: FreeCAD (fully free and open-source), Onshape (browser-based, free public plan), or SolidWorks if you have access. Every block below works in any parametric CAD tool. Check tasks off as you go — this page remembers your progress.

Goal for the day

  1. Sketch with dimensions and constraints (fully-defined sketches).
  2. Turn sketches into solids with extrude, revolve, fillet, chamfer, shell, and patterns.
  3. Model a real-world object from measurements.
  4. Assemble multiple parts with joints/mates.
  5. Export a technical drawing and an STL for 3D printing.

Sketching fundamentals

The single most important CAD skill. Don't rush this block.

Drills — do all three
  1. A 60×40 mm rounded rectangle with 4 symmetric holes.
  2. A "keyhole" slot shape using tangent arcs.
  3. A hex profile centered on the origin, fully defined with 2 dimensions.
CHKEvery drill sketch is fully defined — zero loose entities.
10:30 – 12:00

From sketch to solid

Drills
  1. A dice: cube + filleted edges + patterned dimple cuts.
  2. A simple bracket: L-profile extrusion, mounting holes, fillets at the stress corner.
  3. A pulley or bottle cap using revolve + circular pattern.
CHKYou can predict what each feature will do before you click OK.
12:00 – 12:45

Lunch break

Step away from the screen — spatial reasoning consolidates during rest.

Afternoon — real work
12:45 – 14:45

Model real objects

This is where skills become real. Grab calipers (or a ruler) and model physical objects near you.

Rules for this block
  1. Sketch on the correct plane deliberately; don't just accept the default.
  2. Every sketch fully defined before moving on.
  3. Name your features in the timeline/tree as you go.
CHKObject 3's two parts actually fit each other dimensionally (verify with a section view).

Reference

Key habits to build from day one

HabitWhy it matters
Fully define every sketchPrevents models breaking when edited later.
Sketch centered on originMakes mirroring, patterns, and edits vastly easier.
Name features and componentsYour model tree is documentation.
Model design intentParametric edits should update the whole part correctly, not just the shape.
Fillets lastFillets early in the timeline cause downstream failures.

If you only have 4 hours

Setup (1 h) → sketching drills 1–2 (1 h) → sketch-to-solid drills 1–2 (1 h) → model one real object (45 min) → export an STL (15 min).

Resources

  • Fusion 360: Autodesk's official "Fusion 360 for beginners" learning path; Product Design Online on YouTube — the "Learn Fusion 360 in 30 Days" series.
  • FreeCAD: MangoJelly Solutions beginner series on YouTube; the FreeCAD wiki tutorials.
  • Onshape: Onshape Learning Center self-paced courses — free and excellent.
  • Practice prompts: r/cad and "Too Tall Toby" daily CAD challenges — timed modeling from drawings, ideal from day 2 onward.

After day 1 — where to go next

  1. Week 1: one "Too Tall Toby"-style timed model per day (20–30 min each).
  2. Week 2: lofts, sweeps, and basic surfacing; model something curvy, like a computer mouse.
  3. Week 3: parametric design with user variables and equations; design-for-3D-printing tolerances (clearance fits, snap fits).
  4. Ongoing: pick a real project you'll actually manufacture or print — nothing accelerates CAD like real constraints.
